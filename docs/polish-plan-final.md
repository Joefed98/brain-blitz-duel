## Decisions for Joe (each with a recommendation)

1. **Desktop arcade.** Options A–D are costed in section B. **Recommendation: C, the Cabinet, for every game now; D (Breaker and Rally as landscape games under new stamps) only after Joe has played the Cabinet.** B is not worth building; A makes phones pay for computers.
2. **Difficulty: what "a clear step" means.** Section D proposes a rule: every level changes at least one **mechanic** (a new hazard, a new rule, hidden information, a different board shape), never only a number; Expert adds a second. Where a game's Expert today is "the same game, 20 % faster", the spec names the new mechanic. **Recommendation: approve the rule and the per-game table; Joe's playtest decides each Expert's numbers.** The bot targets (198 / 160 / 140) stay a guide.
3. **"Go back" against the skins, cosmetics and demos built since.** Rule: restore the *look*, keep the *rules*. Every revert in this plan is display only except Moon Climb's ninja size, Fission's atom size and Wavefront's formation, which are new stamps. Skins: none of the reverted pieces is a skinnable one (the skins are Cue Ball, Crazy Golf, Curl Champ, Apex, Top Bins, Full House and the card backs), and the Sports changes here are not drawn in the pinned frames, so no skin digest is re-recorded except Curl Champ's backing strip (C4.9, with Joe's approval). Demos: every intro demo is an input log; a look change never invalidates one; the three new stamps stop their game's demo until the Save-intro-demos workflow re-records it (the fixtures exist). Cosmetics: the Strategy renders being removed (gems, marbles) are game art, not catalogue items; nothing owned changes. **Recommendation: approve the rule.**
4. **Checks that assert the new look.** Fourteen assertion lines say "the new art is there" or "no glow" (section K lists each with its exact lines). They were Joe's 15 Nov requests and this plan reverses them. **Recommendation: Joe approves the list once, here, with the dated note the rules require, and each build session changes only its own lines.**
5. **Things in Joe's notes I think are mistakes.**
   - *Redesigning the avatars in Blender now.* 32 figures with cosmetics composited on the same 100×100 box; a redraw is a month of art and touches every identity fixture. Drop the disc (A3), keep the figures; revisit when the key art exists.
   - *A splash sound on a cold open.* iPhone and Chrome block audio before the first tap. The bolt fills silently on first open and sounds on every re-open (A1). A "Tap to start" splash would allow the sound but adds a tap to every open; not recommended.
   - *Moon Climb's ninja drawn smaller.* Rule 3 (art within 2 px of the hit box) forbids a small figure in the big box; the honest route is a new stamp with a smaller body (C3.5), which is why it sits in the difficulty session.
   - *"Smaller atoms" in Fission* is a hit-box change, so a new stamp (C1.8), not a paint job; the white shine is a paint job (C1.7). Both are in.
   - *Crazy Golf's "old aiming".* The finger mapping never changed; the drawing did. The old dotted aim line and power pill come back (C4.1). Removing the Chip shot would strand holes the bank proves with chips, so it stays.
   - *Wavefront's wider formation* is a gameplay change (hit boxes, spawns, a re-proved deal), so it is a stamp in the difficulty session (C1.6), not an art item.
6. **Scoring review** (section I): nine rows with a recommendation each; two say "leave it".
7. **The order of the build sessions and what Fable builds** (section L): Fable builds the Apex yaw sign (C4.7), the reveal template (G1) and the Cabinet's layout rules (B1's CSS skeleton), the three items most likely to be got wrong twice. Everything else goes to the seven sessions.

## B. Desktop (computer and sideways tablet, 1024 px and up)

### The decision: how arcade games fill a computer screen

Today's rule, "the game never changes on desktop", is enforced by two checks (`arena-world-check`: the same `--arena-w/--arena-h` on every device; `desktop-fairness-check`: a mouse at 1440×900 and a finger at 390×844 reach the sim as the same world points and the same checksum) and by the sims themselves, which never read the layout. At 1440×900 a tall world (most arcade games are 390×730 to 468×868) draws at about 480×900 px between the 76 px rail and the 320 px panel, so ~560 px of the width is the game's fill under a fade. That is the "long, thin phone" Joe sees.

Four options were costed (research in this plan's folder, `desktop-research.md`):

| Option | What a desktop player gets | Fairness in rated play and rooms | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. A wider world for everyone, new stamp per game | A landscape court on every device | Fair (one world), but phones draw the wider world at 390/W (Breaker 0.70 at 560 wide): smaller targets and text on the device most players use; paddle games change feel (longer ball travel) | Per game: new `<game>Rev` with the width in the state, re-fitted curve and bands, re-proved bank or deal (Wavefront, Orbit, Grappler, Tilt, Rainbow Bridge, Ball Tracker, Roofbound, the Sports games), self-replay entry, legacy fixture, intro demo re-recorded, text floor, help text, practice bests restart, recorded rated runs drop for 14 days. L per game |
| B. Desktop-only worlds kept out of rated and Daily play | A wider world in practice only | Rooms, the Daily, challenges and rated play deal one puzzle for everyone, so a desktop player there still gets the column | Everything in A for the variant, plus a level-like variant stamp, separate bests, and both fairness checks rewritten (forbidden) | 
| C. The same world, the space round it designed | The identical world inside a designed cabinet: live stats, the run timeline, standings and chat in a room, scenery carried out, canvas HUDs moved out of the world | Nothing changes; both checks stay green by construction | Front-end and design only: the arena panel, the fills, a `hud: false` renderer flag per game. M |
| D. C for everyone, A for a few | Breaker and Rally (and Blockzone, free) as landscape games | As A for those games | A's cost for each chosen game |

**Recommendation: C now, for every game, as the "Cabinet" layout (mockups D1 and D2); D later only if Joe, having played the Cabinet, still wants Breaker and Rally as landscape games (each is then its own L-sized item under a new stamp).** Reasons: C makes every arcade screen look designed for the computer this month with no stamps, no re-proved banks and no fairness debate; A makes the phone pay for the computer; B improves practice only and needs the fairness checks loosened, which the house rules forbid. Blockzone is the one game whose world is pure renderer layout (the sim is a 10×20 grid), so it gets a true cabinet (well centre, Next and stats beside) inside C at no stamp cost.

## D. Difficulty

The full spec is `docs/polish-plan-final/difficulty-spec.md` (every game: today's four levels with their real numbers, the proposed change, why it is a clear and creative step, and how Expert is proven). This section is the rule and the summary.

### The rule

1. **Every level changes a mechanic, not only a number.** Easy removes or softens one hazard or adds one aid; Hard adds one new hazard, rule or shape; Expert adds a second and takes away an aid. "20 % faster and 10 s shorter" alone is never a level.
2. **Normal stays today's game** (rated play and the Daily deal it; the framework's rule), so a level never retunes Normal.
3. **Expert must be solvable by the best humans**: for arcade games the witness or oracle reaches 200 *under the human limits* (`HUMAN_LIMITS`: 0.25 s to react, 0.15 s to move) on every dealt seed, or the deal is rejected; for puzzles the person-style solver (deduction plus one-step trials, never guessing) finishes every Expert board inside its clock; the elite bot's median under human limits is 120–150 at Expert, 150–175 at Hard. These are guides; Joe's playtest decides.
4. **A level is proved where it is dealt**: a game with a bank gets one bank per level; a game dealt in the sim proves its witness in the sim.
5. Easy is for a first-time player: the elite bot under human limits scores 190+, the "great" bot stars (175+) on a third to a half of runs.
