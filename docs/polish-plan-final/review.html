4Spotlight Recommended

A bright table in a dark arena.

Floodlit’s lit stage, plus light matte pieces on the board puzzles. Worlds, sports and cards get Floodlit’s treatment (they have no dark pieces to flip).

For: The biggest gain where the complaint is: black tiles, chrome pipes, brushed metal. It is chess.com’s formula, and the one Joulz’s own card and sports games already use.

Against: The largest art job: each puzzle family’s piece set is re-rendered.

My recommendation

Direction 4, Spotlight. The games you like least are the ones with dark pieces on a dark board. The ones that already read well (the card games, Top Bins) have light pieces on a coloured surface, and so does chess.com. Spotlight brings the puzzles into line with them and leaves the frame alone.

Two exceptions are part of it. Retro keeps its dark screen and takes direction 2’s glow, because neon on black is what Retro is. Games about light on dark (Lamplighter, Lightbender, Chroma Lines) keep a deep-coloured board.

If you want a smaller first step, pick direction 1. Spotlight is Floodlit plus the new piece sets, so nothing is wasted.

Difficulty: one lever you can see

Every game gets one headline lever a player sees in the first three seconds, and Easy, Normal, Hard and Expert sit on it as one ladder. Normal is today’s game and never changes. Hard and Expert never add an aid. Expert is proven reachable at 200 by the best humans.

Of 83 games: 1 kept, 48 retuned, 34 redesigned, 5 with one new mechanic. The commonest fault today is that Hard looks the same as Normal. Tap a game for its ladder; the full numbers, proofs and files are in the spec.
